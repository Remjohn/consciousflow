01 — FORMAL DEFINITIONS

The Cosine Similarity Formula

cos(θ) = (A · B) / (‖A‖₂ × ‖B‖₂)

Where A · B is the dot product (accumulated projection), and ‖A‖₂ is the L2 norm (magnitude). Cosine Similarity forcefully divides out volume, returning a bounded scalar in [-1, +1] that measures pure directional alignment.

Why not raw distance? Euclidean distance is computationally unstable in high dimensions — a user typing "I feel fake" (tiny magnitude) and a user writing a paragraph about impostor syndrome (massive magnitude) point in the same direction but sit miles apart in raw distance. Cosine measures only trajectory.

03 — POSITIONAL ENCODING DERIVATION

Why Exponential Frequency Scaling?

PE(pos, 2i) = sin(pos / 10000^(2i/d_model))
PE(pos, 2i+1) = cos(pos / 10000^(2i/d_model))

Why not raw integers? Injecting pos = 1, 2, …, 1024 as scalars would give token 1024 a coordinate thousands of times larger than token 1 — destroying gradient stability.

Why multiple frequencies? A single fast sine wave aliases — position 4 and position 16 both land on the same crest. Stacking hundreds of waves at exponentially decreasing speeds creates a unique harmonic fingerprint for every position.

Why alternating sin/cos? This lets attention layers compute relative distances via rotation matrix offsets — mathematically deducing how far apart tokens sit.

04 — THE HIGH-DIMENSIONAL CURSE

Why 0.40 Cosine Is Massive In 768D

In 2D, two random vectors have a reasonable chance of pointing similarly. In 768 dimensions, the sheer volume of empty space means nearly all random vectors are perfectly orthogonal — their cosine similarity defaults to ~0.000.

This is the concentration of measure. Achieving even cos = 0.40 in ℝ⁷⁶⁸ requires staggering, purposeful non-random alignment. What feels "weak" in 2D is actually a massive structural signal in production AI.
