01 — THE CLOCK AND THE SHADOW

Sine and Cosine as Perpendicular Shadows

A Transformer swallows your entire sentence in one instantaneous geometric gulp — every word processed perfectly in parallel. But if the first word and the last word arrive at the same millisecond, how does the model know that "the cat sat on the mat" differs from "the mat sat on the cat"?

The solution is the oldest math humanity used to track time: rotation. Imagine a clock with a single hand sweeping in a perfect circle.

Shine a spotlight from the right — the shadow on the wall moves up and down. That's Sine. Shine a spotlight from the ceiling — the shadow on the floor moves left and right. That's Cosine.

Two perpendicular perspectives of the same rotation. When cos hits 0, sin hits 1. They oscillate perfectly out of phase — providing two non-interfering signals that create an unbreakable cryptographic signature for each token's position.

02 — THE UNIT CIRCLE

The Rosetta Stone of Trigonometry

Draw a circle centered at the origin (0,0) with radius exactly 1. Any vector arrow from the origin to a point on this circle has magnitude exactly 1. The only thing differentiating vectors is the angle θ from the horizontal x-axis.

The coordinates of any point on the unit circle are (cos θ, sin θ). Because the radius is 1, these values are forever trapped between -1 and +1.

This generates the fundamental identity: sin²θ + cos²θ = 1. This isn't a formula to memorize — it's the Pythagorean theorem proving that decomposing a unit vector into horizontal and vertical shadows doesn't create or destroy energy.

03 — COSINE SIMILARITY

Measuring Pure Directional Alignment

If two vectors represent embeddings for two words, how do we know if they mean the same thing? We could measure raw distance — but magnitude distorts this. One word might be a loud whisper, another a quiet shout of the same concept.

Cosine Similarity grabs both vectors, forces them onto the unit circle (stripping magnitude), and measures the cosine of the angle between them. It returns a single number:

  • +1 → Perfect alignment (identical meaning)
  • 0 → Completely independent (orthogonal)
  • -1 → Hostile opposition (opposite meaning)

04 — POSITIONAL ENCODING

Stamping Time Onto Static Vectors

A Transformer doesn't just attach a raw number like "Position 5" to a word. It attaches a combination of sine and cosine waves rotating at different speeds. The high-frequency waves distinguish nearby tokens; the low-frequency waves distinguish distant tokens.

At the extreme edge — if the slowest sine wave completes a full 360° rotation — the model experiences rotational phase shift collapse. Token 8000 gets the same fingerprint as Token 0. The geometry folds on itself and the model's memory hallucinates.
