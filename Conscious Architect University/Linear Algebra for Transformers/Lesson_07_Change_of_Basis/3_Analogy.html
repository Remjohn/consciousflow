The Transfer Domain

True understanding is demonstrated exclusively by performance on structurally identical problems with different surface features. There are no transformer vectors here. Recognize the geometry yourself.

I. The Six-Domain System

The same mathematical object operates across six domains. The geometry is identical. The surface vocabulary is completely different.

The Core Principle

A basis is a set of independent measurement axes. The same object has different coordinate representations in different bases. The choice of basis determines how easy or hard your problem is. Changing basis = matrix multiplication. The object doesn't move.

Why Basis Choice Matters

The Physical Basis hides the answer to a pressing question behind irrelevant axes. Speed without pressing intelligence is chaos. The Tactical Basis makes the answer trivially readable: Pressing=8 and Positioning=9 directly answer the question. Same player. Completely different legibility depending on which basis you read the coordinates in.

The Tactical Basis is the right basis for this decision — not because it's "more accurate," but because its axes align with the question being asked. This is the identical logic that governs why different Transformer attention heads develop different basis directions: each head learns a basis optimized for a specific detection task.

II. Cross-Domain Fidelity

Not all domain analogies share the same mathematical fidelity. A valid basis change requires linearity, invertibility, and independence. Most real-world conversions break at least one of these.

Common Analogy Failures

Failure 1: "Changing the measurement changes the thing." Switching from Physical to Tactical rating systems doesn't change the player. Basis change is purely representational. The vector is invariant; only its description changes.

Failure 2: "Every conversion between representations is a basis change." Non-linear conversions (ingredient→flavor), lossy conversions (Big Five→MBTI), and categorical conversions (continuous→discrete) are NOT proper basis changes.

Failure 3: "The standard basis is 'real' and alternatives are 'artificial.'" The standard basis {[1,0],[0,1]} has no mathematical privilege. The eigenvector basis may be more natural for a given transformation than either.
