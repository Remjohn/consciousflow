The Coordinate Illusion

Real Failure Case — The Basis Collision
In 2023, a production coaching AI began generating identically-structured formal responses to every user — regardless of their emotional state. A crying client got the same phrasing as an excited one. Diagnosis: the "Formality" steering vector had been applied at α = 2.0, and the "Empathy" dimension was not orthogonal to it — so Formality overwrote Empathy at every step. The team had treated two basis vectors as independent when they were not. The model was measuring the same thing twice under different names. The fix required a complete change of basis — discovering the independent axes that actually spanned the coaching behavior space. That is what this lesson teaches.

Before we touch a single matrix, we need to understand the fundamental illusion at the heart of all coordinate systems. The illusion is this: coordinates are not the thing they describe.

You are a football scout. You watch the same 23-year-old midfielder play three matches. You write three scouting reports — each using a different measurement system:

Report 1 — Physical
Speed8/10
Strength6/10
Endurance9/10
Basis: Physical axes
Report 2 — Positional
Offensive7/10
Defensive5/10
Transitional8/10
Basis: Positional axes
Report 3 — Intelligence
Decision-making9/10
Spatial awareness8/10
Anticipation7/10
Basis: Intelligence axes

Three reports. Three entirely different sets of numbers. Same player. The player didn't change between reports. The measurement system changed. Each measurement system is a basis — a set of independent axes that define how you describe something. Switching between them is change of basis. And this is the operation that governs everything from Fourier Transforms to KV-Direct's 27× compression advantage.

Prediction Gate 01 — The Magnitude Trap
The CCP applies two orthogonal steering vectors simultaneously:
Formality = [3, 0]    Empathy = [0, 4]

If dimensions mixed like liquids — linearly combining their magnitudes — the result would have magnitude 7.

What is the actual mathematical magnitude of the combined vector?

The key insight from that trap: orthogonal dimensions do not mix linearly — they mix by Pythagoras. But there's a deeper principle underneath this. Now test whether you've understood it.
